GIZ × Raya: ICT Inclusion Programme for Persons with Disabilities

The target group had only been admitted to ICT education three years earlier. Closed the target at 104%, a cycle ahead of schedule.

Delivered for
Client / DonorGIZ, Raya
LocationEgypt
RoleProject Manager
Team5: 1 Coordinator, 1 Operations, 1 Graphic Designer, 1 Marketing, 1 Partnerships
DurationUnder 1 year · 2 of 3 planned cohorts
ApproachMulti-channel sourcing experiment + peer-referral networks + accessibility policy review
Scale52 of 50 target Persons with Disabilities placed into Egypt's ICT sector

The Objective

Recruit, prepare, and place 50 Persons with Disabilities holding ICT backgrounds across up to three cohorts into Egypt's ICT sector, with a policy review replacing skills identification, and a handbook capturing the method. Disability types in scope: sensory (visual and hearing impairment) and loco-motor.

The Challenge

Inherited after half a year with only 8 of 18 achieved in the first cohort, client satisfaction very low, and frustration ongoing. The root cause was structural: Persons with Disabilities had only been permitted to enter ICT universities three to four years before the project began. The qualified talent pool barely existed yet, and was invisible to every standard sourcing channel.

The Execution

  • Reverse-engineered the first cohort's shortfall: root-caused an 80-to-12 submission-to-acceptance gap to weak CV quality, poor interview readiness, and dependence on a single sourcing channel, and documented every learning before the next cohort began.
  • Started the next cohort early with an unbranded pilot phase, testing organic social, paid social, a webinar, and referral routes against each other before scaling spend into what worked.
  • Rebuilt the campaign intake forms with conditional logic, routing ineligible respondents out before submission so screening time went only to candidates who could actually be placed.
  • Added a two-layer filtration process, individual screening calls plus CV review and track matching, paired with pre-interview coaching and documentation support for the Integrated Services (disability) card, closing the gaps identified in the first cohort.
  • Partnered with NGOs, charities, and Persons with Disabilities communities, and leveraged peer referral inside those communities: mutual connections between people of the same background proved to be the channel that actually worked.
  • Delivered a full accessibility policy review for Raya, verifying that processes, systems, and accommodations across the interview process, calls, and training all met the requirements of Persons with Disabilities, and documented the entire method in a handbook so equivalent programmes can be executed in future without me.

Results Delivered

  • Programme target closed at 104%: 52 of 50 Persons with Disabilities placed, from a starting point of 8 of 18 in the first cohort.
  • Target reached within two cohorts instead of the three planned, ending the programme a cycle early.
  • Candidates accepted as a share of those submitted rose from 15% in the first cohort to 59% in the second, on roughly 3.5x the registration volume.
  • Accessibility policy review delivered to Raya covering the entire candidate journey.
  • Replication handbook produced and handed over.
  • Client satisfaction recovered from a low base.
